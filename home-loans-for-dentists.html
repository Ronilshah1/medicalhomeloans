Ask us about LMI waivers for dentists

What an LMI waiver is worth.

LMI is normally charged when you borrow more than 80% of a property’s value. For an eligible dentist, a waiver means buying sooner with a smaller deposit, without handing thousands to an insurer that protects the bank.

Dental income, assessed properly.

Most dentists aren’t paid a simple salary, and that’s where standard applications go wrong:

  • Associates on a percentage of billings. Often paid via a service agreement and ABN. Some lenders assess this on a short history; others want two full years of tax returns.
  • Practice owners. Income flowing through a company or trust needs add-backs and the right structure to show your real capacity.
  • Recent graduates. High income growth in the first few years, often alongside HECS/HELP debt, is better understood by some lenders than others.
  • Buying into a practice. Planning your home loan around a practice purchase can protect your borrowing power for both.

FAQ

Home loans for dentists: common questions

Can dentists get a home loan without LMI?

Many lenders waive LMI for AHPRA-registered dentists, typically up to 90% of the property value and sometimes higher. Eligibility, maximum LVR and any minimum income differ by lender.

Do dental hygienists and oral health therapists qualify for an LMI waiver?

Usually not. Most lenders limit their waiver to registered dentists and dental specialists. Hygienists, therapists and prosthetists can still benefit from a broker who finds lenders that treat their income favourably.

I’m an associate paid through an ABN. Can I still borrow?

Yes. Some lenders will assess dental contractor income on a shorter trading history than standard self-employed borrowers. We’ll match you with one that does.

Can I buy a home while I’m buying into a practice?

It’s possible, but the order and structure matter. We’ll plan both so one doesn’t cap your borrowing for the other.
